The model · Free

Joints and members, connected the way they are built.

A frame is any structure you can describe as joints connected by members, each carrying axial force, shear in two directions, torsion and bending about two axes. Global Z is up, the same vertical FoundaTrix Studio and PileAxis use, so a structure and its foundations in one job read each other's directions without conversion.

Members

Free

Entered whole, divided for you

  • A member is drawn end to end; the analysis divides it at every joint on its centre line and every crossing, and reports it whole
  • Crossings connect, or pass unconnected, as the model chooses (an X-brace not bolted at its middle)
  • Beams, columns, vertical and horizontal braces, each type with its own drawing colour and design role; braces pinned at both ends
  • Local axes from global Z or a reference joint, turned by an angle, the section drawn as the analysis bends it
  • A member can be made inactive: drawn, and left out of the analysis

Boundary conditions

Free

Supports, releases and rigid parts

  • Each of a joint's six degrees free, fixed, on a spring, or on a compression-only or tension-only spring
  • Settlements and rotations imposed on held supports per load case
  • Each member end releasing any of its six forces, or partially fixed through a rotational spring
  • Rigid end offsets, results reported face to face, and rigid links tying a joint to its master
  • Tension-only and compression-only members, found by iterating the set of members that work

The Studio

Free

Drafting precision in 3D

  • Joints placed and members drawn on a work plane on XY, YZ or ZX, at any named elevation, with a 2D view of the plane
  • Set-out grid lines with bubbles, a drafting grid laid out by spacing or listed increments, skewed if need be
  • Object snaps, quarter and third points among them, typed coordinates and relative distances
  • Move, copy, array, rotate, mirror and scale; split, merge, extend, trim, match properties, relabel, merge coincident joints
  • Select by criteria read from the objects' own properties, and saved selections kept with the model

Sections and materials

Free

Every property from the AISC database

  • W, M, S, C, MC, L, WT, rectangular and round HSS and pipe, from the AISC Shapes Database v16.0, every property the database's own value
  • ASTM A992, A572 Grade 50, A36, A500 Grade C, A1085 and A53 Grade B, or a general material of your own
  • Section sets: members that share a section, a material and design rules, sized together
  • A section stated by its properties alone, for a member that is not designed

Checking the model

Free

Fifty checks before the first run

  • Errors the analysis would refuse: a part standing on no support, a member of no length, a release set that leaves a member a mechanism, a load placed off its member
  • Warnings where it would run but probably not as meant: two joints a hair apart, two members along one line, members passing close without meeting, a joint nothing holds along an axis, a load drawn twice
  • Each finding coded and listed in the Validation pane, the joint or member it names selected with a click, every distance in your units

Loads · Free

Every load applied exactly where it acts.

Loads are entered in basic load cases, as many as the structure needs, each in its ASCE 7-22 load category: dead, live, roof live, snow, rain, wind or earthquake. A lateral case is reversible along an axis, as an earthquake is, or one way, as a wind case is.

Kinds of load

Free

At joints, along members, over areas

  • Joint forces and moments; member point loads and moments, in global or local axes
  • Uniform or linearly varying loads over all or part of a member, per length or projected, so snow per unit of plan puts nothing on a column
  • Thermal loads: a uniform change, and a difference between opposite faces
  • Self-weight from each section's area and its material's unit weight, with a factor along each axis
  • A load inside a member is never moved to a joint: its effect on the member's ends is found exactly

Area loads

Free

A pressure over a region, spread to its members

  • Regions cornered at joints, so they move with the frame, on a floor, a roof or a wall
  • One way, in the span you state, or two way, each side taking the part of the panel nearest it
  • Delivered as exact distributed loads; what the members carry sums to the pressure times the area
  • A side of a panel with no member on it reported with the load it would have carried

Load combinations · Free

ASCE 7-22, written out for you, traced to its rows.

A combination is a list of terms, each a basic case, a whole category or another combination, with a factor. Each states whether it is solved, how it is analysed, its basis (LRFD, ASD or service), its notional loads and whether the steel is designed from it.

The generator

Free

Sections 2.3 and 2.4, every choice expanded

  • Strength and allowable stress combinations for the categories the model uses, atmospheric ice when asked
  • "Lr or S or R" made one combination per choice, each lateral case in each of its senses
  • The redundancy factor ρ, the vertical seismic effect Ev = 0.2 SDS D and the orthogonal 100%/30% rule when asked
  • Service combinations for deflections, and a row that repeats another left out and counted
  • Each named after its row and direction: ASCE 7-22 2.3.1 5a, W -X, notional -X

Notional loads

Free

Out-of-plumbness, from the combination's own gravity

  • Ni = 0.002 α Yi at every level, α = 1.0 for LRFD and 1.6 for ASD
  • Every downward load given a horizontal twin where it stands, so each level's notional load is its own gravity load's
  • Gravity-only combinations written in four directions; wind and earthquake combinations in the direction of their load
  • The permission of C2.2b(d) to limit them to gravity combinations, offered as a choice that is yours to justify

Analysis · Free

The stiffness method, to first or second order, per combination.

The whole structure's stiffness is assembled from its members and factorised once; every basic case is a substitution and every first-order combination a weighted sum. A combination marked for second order is solved on its own, because second-order results do not add.

Direct analysis

Free

AISC 360-22 Chapter C, as written

  • Each member cut into short pieces with the stiffness its axial force gives it: P-Δ and P-δ both included
  • 0.80 times the axial, shear and torsional stiffness and 0.80 τb times the flexural stiffness, τb from each member's own axial force
  • Iterated until the axial forces and the stiffness agree; a structure that buckles is reported unstable, naming where
  • ASD analysed at 1.6 times the combination and divided back, as Section C2.1(d) requires
  • Or an elastic second-order analysis at full stiffness, for the actual displacements under factored loads

One-way members and supports

Free

What works, found by iteration

  • A tension-only brace compressed, or a compression-only strut pulled, lets go for the next pass; one that has let go takes hold again when its ends move back
  • Compression-only springs for a footing that may lift, tension-only springs as their mirror
  • The passes repeat until nothing changes, and the result lists what let go
  • A structure left unable to stand is reported unstable, naming what let go

The equilibrium audit

Free

Every combination checked against statics, independently of the solver

  • The reactions summed against the loads, notional loads and self-weight included, in force and in moment about the origin
  • A combination passes when what is left is no more than a millionth of the larger of its loads and its reactions
  • One that fails is reported and shown with no result: this is what tells you a result is in equilibrium, rather than that a solver stopped
  • Results belong to the model they came from: any change the analysis reads puts them out of date until the next run

Results · Free

Nothing interpolated between joints.

A member's forces and deflections are reported at its joints and at points between them no more than six inches apart, each exact for the analysis. The forces follow by statics from the member's end forces and its loads; the deflections by integrating its curvature in closed form. In a first-order combination, each force's largest and least value along a member is found exactly, wherever it falls.

  • The deformed shape of any combination, magnified as you choose, and each member coloured by its axial force, shear, torsion, moment or deflection
  • Any force's diagram drawn on every member at once, a moment on its compression face, under one combination or as the envelope of them all, with the values written
  • The Member Diagram of one member: its six forces along it under each combination, and their envelope
  • Reactions, displacements, member forces, deflections with L/Δ, and the reaction and member envelopes with the combination that gave each value

Steel design · Free

AISC 360-22, LRFD and ASD, at every station.

Every run designs the steel from that run's own forces, at the very points the results report, so a design never analyses the structure again. A steel design is made from combinations analysed by the direct analysis method, so the required strengths include second-order effects and K is 1.0.

What is checked

Free

Chapters D to H, clause by clause

  • Tension (D), compression (E), flexure about both axes (F), shear both ways (G), combined forces and torsion (H)
  • Every ratio with its limit state and its clause; a member's governing check is its largest under any combination
  • Each combination designed by its own basis, so a model may carry LRFD and ASD combinations at once
  • A single angle in an X panel checked as cross bracing (E5)

Lengths

Free

Unbraced lengths found from the structure

  • A member is held where another member or brace frames in and reaches a support by another way
  • Lcx and Lcy across its own local axes, the torsional length, and Lb segment by segment
  • Cb by Equation F1-1 from each segment's quarter-point moments; 1.0 out to a free end
  • Any of them stated member by member instead, in the Design Parameters table

Choosing sections

Free

Optimise: the lightest that passes

  • For each section set marked Optimise, the lightest of its group (every W16 for a W16X26) that passes every check
  • The structure analysed again with what was chosen, until an analysis changes nothing
  • A set that has grown is never made lighter again, so the choice cannot go back and forth
  • Nothing applied until you say so, then as one step you can undo

Deflection

Free

Net deflection, as a beam deflects

  • Each beam under each service combination that states a limit, L/360 or as you set
  • Measured against the points that hold it, so a settling support or a swaying frame is not counted against the beam
  • A member that fails drawn whole in the colour of its ratio
  • ASCE MOP 113 deflection classes A, B and C for substation structures

Pro

Tapered tubular poles, to ASCE 48-19.

A transmission pole's shaft, an H-frame's poles and a davit arm are tapered tubes braked from plate. Strutrix Studio analyses them as the tubes they are and checks them by ASCE/SEI 48-19, or a round tube by AISC 360-22 if you choose.

Pro

The tube's own section, at every station.

  • Round, or a regular polygon of an even number of flats, the diameter varying in a straight line from base to tip
  • Sharp-cornered properties by ASCE 48-19 Appendix B; the analysis in one-foot steps, each the tube's section at its middle
  • Local buckling by Table 5-1, the combined stress at every point of the section with the shear where it acts, tension, bending, shear and torsion, and truss members
  • Under every strength combination analysed second-order, on factored loads, every check citing its equation
  • Generate a pole in shafts with slip joints and davit arms, or an H-frame with its cross-arm and X-brace, wires attached
  • Optimise gives each tube the thinnest plate that passes, keeping its diameters

Line loading · Free

The loads a line puts on its structure, by its standard.

From three tables, the weather cases, the wires and each wire's tension in each weather case, the line loading writes the load cases, the loads and the combinations a transmission line puts on a pole, an H-frame or a substation structure. What it writes is ordinary loads you can read, change or delete.

NESC

IEEE C2-2023

Rule 250B's combined ice and wind districts, Rule 250C's extreme wind and Rule 250D's extreme ice with concurrent wind, by grade of construction.

ASCE MOP 74

Line structural loading

Wind at the speed of the return period chosen, and radial glaze ice with concurrent wind.

ASCE MOP 113

Substation structures

Extreme wind and ice with concurrent wind, combined by Table 3-18 or 3-19, and the deflection loading its classes are checked under.

Wires and insulators

Conductors and ground wires with their diameters, weights, bundles, spans, line angle and insulators, attached at joints.

Wind on the members

Each weather case's wind on the structure's own members, by height above the ground, with the exposure and topographic factor.

Any yaw

The wind's angle to the line from square to along it, and wind both ways reversed through 180 degrees.

Pro · The structure link

Reactions handed to your foundations, live.

A save after a run carries each support's reactions under each basic case and each combination solved. FoundaTrix Studio and PileAxis, in the same job, read them as their loads, always from the latest saved version, and keep no copy.

  • FoundaTrix Studio: each support's reactions by basic case, grouped by category and combined by its own foundation combinations, or your combinations as they are; and a footing placed at each support
  • PileAxis: one support's reactions, under a combination or a basic case, as the loads at a pile's head
  • Each analysis there records the version it read, and says so when the structure has changed since
  • A structure changed since its run is saved without reactions, so nothing ever reads reactions of another model

Reading a structure is a Pro feature of FoundaTrix Studio and PileAxis and needs a Strutrix Studio subscription or trial as well.

Limits

What version 1 does not do, said plainly.

So you know what still needs doing elsewhere. The user's manual lists every assumption and every limit.

Members only

No plates, walls or solids. A floor or a wall is represented by the loads it delivers to the members.

Static and elastic

No modal, response-spectrum or time-history analysis, and no material nonlinearity: members are linearly elastic, with small displacements.

No building wind or seismic generators

Wind and earthquake load cases on a building are entered as loads; the line loading writes a line's wind on its wires and structure.

Direct analysis for design

The amplified first-order method of Appendix 8 is not offered: a strength combination analysed to first order is not designed, and the program says so.

Steel members only

No concrete, wood, cold-formed steel or aluminium design, and no connection or base plate design.

No sag-tension

A wire's tensions are the line designer's. No guys, broken-wire or construction cases, and no lattice towers.

Validation

Pinned to answers it did not write.

Every result is held to an independent answer, a closed form, a published benchmark or a worked example, by an automated test, never to the program's own earlier output alone.

AISC Commentary

Second-order benchmarks

The second-order analysis reproduces the Specification Commentary's benchmark problems for a cantilever column and a pinned column under axial load and lateral load.

AISC Design Examples

Member strengths

Checks against the Design Examples' LRFD and ASD columns: a W18X50 braced at its third points gives φMn of about 307 kip-ft where the example, rounding Cb, prints 305.

Closed form

Supports and releases

A fixed-ended beam whose support settles carries 12EIΔ/L³ and 6EIΔ/L²; one that turns, 4EIθ/L and 2EIθ/L; a determinate one tilts and carries nothing.

Statics

Equilibrium, every time

Every combination's reactions balance its loads within a millionth of their size, and a printed report's reactions are held to the loads its factors put on the frame, worked by hand.

ASCE 48-19

Tube properties

The closed forms of Appendix B held to an independent integration of the outlines, and to every round HSS of the AISC database.

Citations

Every check names its clause

A design check written without the clause it comes from fails a test, so every ratio you read can be traced to the page.
