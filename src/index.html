3D frame analysis & steel design · Windows

Steel frames in 3D, analysed and designed end to end.

Strutrix Studio analyses and designs three-dimensional frames: buildings, industrial structures, pipe racks, towers, trusses and transmission poles. It solves every load combination to first order or by the AISC 360-22 direct analysis method, checks every steel member by LRFD or ASD, chooses the lightest sections that pass, and audits every result's equilibrium on its own.

Any frame, in three dimensions

Draw it member by member, or generate it from a few dimensions.

Anything you can describe as joints and members, in any arrangement in space. The Generate menu builds the common ones whole, with their supports, as one step you can undo, and what it builds is an ordinary model you edit like any other.

What it does, free

Every member, under every combination, from one model.

Build the structure once. Every load combination is analysed by the method you choose for it, every steel member is checked at points no more than six inches apart under every combination designed, and every result is audited for equilibrium by a separate calculation.

Model

Draw it as it is built, with drafting precision.

Enter each member end to end, however many others meet it along the way: the program divides it at every joint on its line and every crossing, and reports it whole. Set-out grid lines, work planes on any floor, snaps and typed coordinates put every joint where it belongs.

  • Supports fixed, pinned or held degree by degree, on springs or one-way springs, and settlements imposed per load case
  • End releases and partial fixity, rigid end offsets and rigid links, tension-only and compression-only members
  • Move, copy, array, rotate, mirror, scale, split, merge, extend and trim, each one step you can undo
  • The model checks itself before it runs, naming the joint or member: a part on no support, a near miss, a load drawn twice

Loads

Loads where they act. Combinations by ASCE 7-22.

Loads go in basic load cases, each in its ASCE 7-22 category. A load inside a member is applied exactly where it is, never moved to a joint, and an area load is spread over the members around each panel, one way or two, as exact distributed loads.

  • Joint, point and distributed loads, projected loads, area loads, thermal loads and self-weight
  • The ASCE 7-22 strength and allowable stress combinations generated, each named after its row
  • Every lateral case in each of its senses, with ρ, the vertical seismic effect and the 100%/30% rule when asked
  • Notional loads worked from each combination's own gravity loads, as the direct analysis method asks
  • ASCE 7-22 §2.3
  • §2.4
  • §12.4.2.2
  • §12.5
  • AISC 360-22 C2.2b

Analysis

Second order where it matters: the direct analysis method.

Each combination says how it is analysed. A first-order combination is the factored sum of its basic cases, exact and almost free. A direct analysis combination is solved on its own, the members cut into short pieces so the sway of the joints and the bowing of each member are both included.

  • The stiffness reduced as AISC 360-22 requires: 0.80 for axial and shear, 0.80 τb for flexure
  • ASD analysed at 1.6 times the combination and divided back, so it sees the same amplification
  • A combination that buckles the structure is reported as unstable, naming where, never with a result
  • Forces and deflections exact between joints, every six inches, nothing interpolated
  • AISC 360-22 C2
  • C2.1(d)
  • C2.3
  • C3

Steel design

Every steel member checked to AISC 360-22, clause by clause.

Every run designs the steel from that run's own forces: tension, compression, flexure about both axes, shear both ways, and combined forces and torsion, at points no more than six inches apart, by LRFD or ASD as each combination states.

  • The lengths a member buckles over found from the structure itself, Lcx, Lcy, Lb and Cb by Equation F1-1, or stated by you
  • Each ratio with its limit state and the clause that states it
  • Optimise: the lightest section of each group that passes, analysed again until the choice settles
  • A beam's net deflection under each service combination that states a limit, L/360 and the like
  • AISC 360-22 Ch. D to H
  • F1-1
  • E5
  • AISC Shapes Database v16.0

Results

Results you read on the model, and in tables you can sort.

Step through the combinations on the deformed shape, colour every member by a force, or draw a force's diagram on every member at once, under one combination or as the envelope of them all, with the largest values written where they stand.

  • The Member Diagram of any member under each combination, its governing check marked along it
  • Reactions, displacements, member forces, deflections and their envelopes as sheets that sort, filter and copy
  • Results go out of date the moment you change what the analysis reads, and say so

An audit that does not trust the solver

Each combination's reactions are summed against its loads, notional loads and self-weight included, and a combination that does not balance is shown with no result.

Built for large structures

Thousands of members and hundreds of combinations, solved on every core your computer has, with a sparse solver for structures wide in every direction.

Lines, poles and substations

Weather cases by the NESC, ASCE MOP 74 or MOP 113 written as wire loads, wind on the members and combinations, and MOP 113 deflection classes checked.

Spreadsheet in, spreadsheet out

Joints, members, sections, loads and combinations are tables too: paste a block from a spreadsheet, or copy any result table out.

Pro

Transmission poles and H-frames, to ASCE 48-19.

A section may be a tapered tube formed from plate, round or a regular polygon of an even number of flats (6, 8, 12 and 16 are usual), its diameter varying from one end of the member to the other. The analysis takes it in one-foot steps, each the tube's own section, and the design checks it at every station.

  • The sharp-cornered properties of ASCE 48-19 Appendix B, at every diameter along the tube
  • Local buckling, combined stress at every point of the section, shear and torsion, and truss members, to Chapter 5
  • Generate a pole with its shafts and davit arms, or an H-frame with its cross-arm and X-brace, wires attached
  • Optimise gives each tube the thinnest plate that passes, keeping the diameters its clearances set
Pro

IFC 4.3 and Revit, from the structure you designed.

Send the structure to any BIM tool as IFC 4.3, or into Revit as structural columns and framing through the Ramisoft Revit add-in. Every member is written at its centre line as its true steel profile, turned as the analysis turns it.

  • Each member's section, set, type, length and base quantities, and its design result where the design still describes the model
  • The set-out grid, and the site's origin where you want it
  • The same identities on every export, so a coordination tool replaces a member rather than adding a copy
  • Every file read back before it replaces the last one, with a summary of what was written

Pro · The structure link

Your foundations read the reactions. Nobody retypes them.

Run the structure and save it to the cloud: its support reactions travel with it. In the same job, FoundaTrix Studio and PileAxis read them as their loads, always from the latest saved version.

  • FoundaTrix Studio reads each support's reactions by load case, grouped by category, and combines them by its own foundation combinations; or takes your combinations as they are, second-order effects included
  • It can place a footing under each support
  • PileAxis reads one support's reactions, under a combination or a basic case, as the loads at a pile's head
  • Each analysis there records the version it read; when the structure has changed since, the next run says so

Reactions are handed over as the loads on the foundation, in kips and kip-feet along the global axes. Only a structure saved with a current run can be read. Reading a structure is a Pro feature of FoundaTrix Studio and PileAxis and needs a Strutrix Studio subscription or trial as well.

Your own AI assistant

Describe the building to your AI assistant. It builds the frame.

Connect an AI assistant that accepts custom MCP connectors, Claude among them, and tell it the bays, the storeys, the sections, the supports and the loads. It creates the building frame in your cloud job with Strutrix Studio's own generators and checks.

  • Made exactly as Generate > Building Frame and the ASCE 7-22 combination generator make it
  • A value you did not give is asked for, and every value supplied for you is listed back
  • Saved unrun, in your name: open it, review it, run it and confirm the results yourself
  • It also searches the user's manual, and finds your structures among your jobs by what they hold

How to connect your assistant is explained in the program's user's manual (Help > User's Manual). It works as you, with your own account. Creating a frame needs only an account; a search by what a structure holds needs Pro.
